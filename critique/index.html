STATS 401: Data Acquisition and Visualization

Visualization Critique and Redesign

1. Navigraph's FlySim Survey

I took this visualization from Navigraph's 2025 - 2026 survey on flight simulator users. Specifically, this visualization look into how users switch between different simulators during the year with a Sankey diagram. It help to understand the landscape of the flight simulator market and how it changed over the year.

Source:

  1. Survey blog
  2. Full survey PDF
  3. Dataset

2. Analyze the Original Visualization

Intended message: The original visualization communicates how users switch between flight simulators. The plot from the full report (left) shows the full status of all the users who have switched simulators, while the plot from the blog (right) only highlighted the users who migrated from MSFS2020. The trend is that most of these users moved to MSFS2024, and only a fraction of them moved X-Plane 12 and other simulators. Context: MSFS2024 is the latest flight simulator by Microsoft, however its quality wasn't good at initial release and lacked third-party plugins. The fact that many MSFS2020 users are migrating to the latest version suggests that the MSFS2024 has become more stable and the plugin ecosystem has improved greatly. This is important message for flight simulator users who also consider updating to MSFS2024.

Audience: Flight simulator users; Marketing teams in the field.

Tasks: Compare how the market shares of different simulator changed over time; Identify the migration trend for users of different simulators.

3. Critique the Visualization

Strengths (Blog version, right)

  1. Sankey diagrams is a good choice for the task of identifying migration trends, which clearly shows the propotion of users migrating to each individual simulator.
  2. The design choice to highlight migration from MSFS2020 answers the question of many MSFS2020 users on whether they should upgrade to MSFS2024. Also, the highlight avoids cluttering and keeps the diagram clean and readable.

Weaknesses

The weakenesses are with the expressiveness - it fails to fully represent data and answer users' potential questions.

  1. Expressiveness #1 - This static visualization cannot capture the full migration landscape. If we try to pack all migration paths into the Sankey diagram, we run into hairballs (left). This prevents the user to explore the complete trend. This can be easily fixed with interactive visualization with D3.
  2. Expressiveness #2 - This visualization fails to capture the users who stick to their current simulator over the year, losing important information on customer loyalty. It also fails to account for the changes of market shares of different simulator. Both are important for understanding the market of flight simulators. This can be fixed by adding a new view that shows the status for all users.
  3. Expressiveness #3 - Advanced users to inspect the exact propotion of the users who switched and stayed on a simulator - not provided in current visualization. The fix is to add a second table to show detailed data based on user query.

4. Redesign

How to read this

  • Each simulator has two bars: on the left, where its users came from; on the right, where they are now. Bar height is that simulator's share of the respondents, so a bar that shrinks from left to right lost share. The ribbons between are people who moved.
  • Hover to preview, click to pin, Esc to clear.
  • A legend chip traces one simulator on both sides. A bar traces one side only — the left bar shows where its users went, the right bar shows where its users came from.

5. Design Justification

  1. Added interactive highlight & pin features to fix Expressiveness #1. This allows better inspection of "where are the users coming from" and "where are the users going to". The users are able to clearly see and extract the information they wanted.
  2. Added the "all respondents" view to fix Expressiveness #2, allowing users to check and compare the loyalty of different simulators as well as the changes in market shares. We can see that MSFS2024 is gaining momentum, but a considerable number (~30%) of users still stick with MSFS2020. - Important insight for Microsoft's marketing team.
  3. Added detailed table to fix Expressiveness #3. Importantly, the table is coordinated with highlight & pin - when nothing is selected, it displays overview stats. When the use pin a specific simulator itself / links to it / links from it, the table displays specific migration stats. This level of detail design make sure the user can always extract target data directly.

Overall, this improved design keeps the readability of the original MSFS2020 migration diagram while significantly improves expressiveness to support better analysis of the flight simulator market. Moreover, the level of detail designs through the highlight & pin + dynamnic table implementation allow user to easily switch between overviews and details. However, this visualzation has higher information density and could take the users longer to process.
