3. 2025 GDP Cartogram

Here, the GDP is encoded by Area instead - see the scale on bottom-left corner. The background continents and coordinated interactions with the Choropleth are added so that users can have a clearer mental picture.

4. Design and Comparison

  1. Which countries are most visually prominent in each map? - In the Choropleth, countries with the largest areas are the most prominant visually (for example, Russia); The area encodes the GDP instead in the Cartogram, so the countries with the highest GDPs are the most prominant visually (for example, China and USA).
  2. Which countries become much larger or smaller in the cartogram? - Russia and Brazil have become much smaller, while South Korea and Japan have become much larger, indicating a mismatch between geographical area and GDP.
  3. What geographic information does the choropleth preserve? - Relative position and common borders of countries.
  4. What geographic information does the cartogram distort? - Areas and shapes of countries.
  5. What types of questions or tasks can the choropleth best support? - Questions that are related to geographical information, for example, "What part of Asia is most developed / underdeveloped in terms of GDP?". These questions will be harder solely based on the distorted cartogram - that's also why I build the coorindated interaction between the two maps and tooltips here.
  6. What types of questions or tasks can the cartogram best support? - Comparing the GDPs of different contries, as the differences in sizes makes the skewed GDP distribution prominant. Also, the differences in size is easier to identify compare to differences in colors for such a skewed distribution.
  7. How does changing from color encoding to area encoding change your perception of global economic size? - This change highlights the unequal development of different countries - emphasized by the very different areas. The distorted shapes also make me curious and encourage me to look into the data.
